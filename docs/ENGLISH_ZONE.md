# English Street — plan & script

A new zone where he learns **English through the vehicles he already loves**. He speaks Vietnamese, can't read, and is 3½–5. This doc covers the teaching approach, the story, the word list, every game with its levels, the spoken script (VI + EN), the code changes, and the order to build things in.

> Status: **built** (2026-11-13), then **redesigned around visible levels** the same day. What is built is described in [GAME_DESIGN.md → English Street](GAME_DESIGN.md) and that section wins wherever this plan differs. In short:
> - **English Street is a road.** Six towns (rescue cars, construction, on the street, colors, go and stop, far away), each with one stop per level (★ ship / ★★ / ★★★ Zip's Garage or Zip Says) and a badge; the ship, Zip's Garage and Zip Says are what happens at a stop, not separate games.
> - **Three star levels per town decide the help:** ★ meaning + English word · ★★ a short frame + the word (no meaning) · ★★★ just the word. Level ups are announced; the ★★★ stop gives the town's badge and opens the next town. The per-word boxes and timers in §5 are gone (a word just keeps the stars of the highest level he got it right at).
> - **One language at a time:** Zip, the classmates and the helper speak the app language; English is only the word being taught, said on its own.
> - Classmates (§8) are built as in-game characters: naming (mic / letters / ready names), the friend bar, reactions, tap jokes, sleepy Bear.
>
> Sections below are the original plan and are kept for the ideas not built yet (units 4, 6–10, Fix-it Zip, Paint it!, Order Up, friend turns…). The house rules in [GAME_DESIGN.md](GAME_DESIGN.md) still apply.

## 1. The big idea in one paragraph

A small blue tow truck called **Zip** has just come off a big ship at the port, carrying English words from far away. He helps Zip, and Zip teaches him the words. Zip talks to him in his own language; **only the words being taught are English, each said on its own**. Every English word is either **a vehicle, a part of a vehicle, something a vehicle does, how a vehicle looks, or where a vehicle is**, so every word sits inside his passion. A **Vietnamese helper voice** (the narrator who already runs the app) explains new words, then **steps back word by word** as he learns them, until Zip only has to say the English word. Each finished set of words opens a new building on English Street (fire station, construction site, car wash…), so the street grows as he learns.

## 2. How a 3–5 year old picks up a second language (the rules we design by)

| # | Principle | What it means in the game |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Understanding comes before speaking.** Young children often stay quiet in a new language for months (the "silent period"). That's normal. | Almost every game asks him to **tap or act**, not to talk. Speaking is invited but never graded. |
| 2 | **Meaning from the picture, not from translation.** | An English word is always said **while its picture or action is on screen**. Vietnamese gives help; it doesn't replace the picture. |
| 3 | **The "sandwich", fading per word.** | New word: *VI → **EN** → VI*. Known word: *VI frame + **EN** word*. Mastered word: *English only*. Help fades **per word**, not per game (§5). |
| 4 | **Many meetings, in many places.** A word needs ~10–20 meaningful encounters. | The same word shows up in 4–6 different games, in the song, and as an echo in the old games (§9). |
| 5 | **Words come in chunks and actions** (Total Physical Response). | Teach *"Go!", "Stop!", "Beep beep!", "Help, please!", "Thank you!"* as whole chunks that make something happen on screen. |
| 6 | **One language at a time; English only for the words.** | Instructions, cheers, greetings and "try again" are in his language. The English word is always said on its own, never inside a sentence of the other language. |
| 7 | **Short and every day beats long and rare.** | One "Drive with Zip" session is about 6–8 minutes and introduces at most 3 new words. |
| 8 | **Keep Vietnamese strong.** | The Vietnamese helper voice stays warm and present. English is added on top, never shames the home language. |

## 3. Story & characters

- **Zip** 🚛: a small blue tow truck with big eyes and a hook. Cheerful, a bit clumsy (his parts "break", the kid fixes them). Speaks the child's language in a higher-pitched character voice; when he teaches, he says just the English word.
- **The helper** 🗣️: the app's usual Vietnamese narrator (or Dad's recorded voice). Explains, encourages, translates *only while it's needed*.
- **Visiting vehicles**: every unit brings new friends off the ship: a fire truck, a digger, a motorbike… Each speaks its own sound plus its English name.
- **Classmates** 🐻🐱🐶🐰: 2–4 little friends he names himself. They ride along in every English game in their small cars, cheering, joking, asking for his help and reacting to everything he does (§8).
- **English Street** 🏘️: the zone screen is a street with **locked buildings shown as grey shadows**. Finishing a unit lights one up (§6). This is the long-term motivation: *"What will arrive on the ship next?"*

Why Zip: a friendly character who brings the words gives a **reason** to learn them, and makes him the **helper**, which kids this age love, rather than the one being tested.

**Phrases threaded through every unit** (taught by repetition, not as a unit): *Hello! · Bye bye! · Let's go! · Ready, set, go! · My turn! · Your turn! · Help, please! · Thank you! · Uh-oh! · Wow! · Yes! · No! · Good job! · High five!*

**Sentence ladder** (Level 3, from unit 3 on): *It's a bus.* → *It's a **red** bus.* → *The bus is **big**.* → *The digger can **dig**.* → *The car is **under** the bridge.*

**Brand hook:** he already knows the brand names, and they sound the same in English. At L3 the brands carry the new words: *"Find the **white** Toyota!"*, *"The Ferrari is **fast**!"*, *"**Three** BMWs!"*.

This works like the existing per-game rows (level picker + tick box to hide). Settings → **English Street** has one row per unit:

```
 …

```

- **Auto** (the default): the unit opens by itself when the unit before it is done (or when every earlier unit is switched off). Until then its building is a grey shadow.
- **On**: open now, whatever the order. Use it to jump to something he is excited about this week, like motorbikes after a ride.
- **Off**: the unit is gone. Its building disappears from the street, its words are never asked or used as distractors, and its chant and games skip it. **Word progress is kept**, so switching it back on carries on where he left off.
- At least one unit always stays on. Switching off the last one shows a short note instead.
- Each row shows the unit's progress (stars and *known / total*). Tapping the row opens its word list, with a ✓ *He said it!* button per word (§11).
- The individual games can still be hidden with the usual game tick boxes. A unit switch controls the **words**; a game tick box controls the **activity**.
- There's a **Reset English progress** button at the bottom. It confirms first, and keeps the switches and the classmates (names, colors, extras).

## 5. Word mastery & fading the Vietnamese

Each word has a **box 0–4** (a Leitner system), saved on the device:

| Box | Stage | How the word is spoken | Moves when… |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **New** | not yet met | the word is shown in *Ship Arrives* → box 1 |
| 1–2 | **Meeting** (A) | VI word → **EN word ×2** → VI encouragement: *"Xe cứu hỏa — **fire truck**! **Fire truck**!"* | right on the first try → +1 · a miss → −1 (never below 1) |
| 3 | **Knowing** (B) | VI frame, **EN word**: *"Tìm **fire truck** nào!"* | same |
| 4 | **Mastered** (C) | **Just the English word**, said by Zip: *"Fire truck!"* | a miss drops it to 3 |

- **Which words get asked:** each round picks its target from the active unit, weighted to boxes 1–2 (≈60%), 3 (≈30%) and 4 (≈10%, as review from earlier units). Distractors come from words he has *met*, so he is never asked to pick between two unknown words.
- **New words per session: at most 3.**
- **Word cards:** a word reaching box 3 earns a **word card** in My Garage → 📚 *My Words*. At box 4 the card turns gold ⭐.
- **Grown-ups override** (settings → English help): **Auto** (per word, the default) · **Always help** (stage A for everything) · **English only** (stage C for everything: just the English words, no Vietnamese help — for when a grown-up is sitting with him).
- If the app language is already English, everything is simply spoken in English and stages only change how often a word repeats.

## 6. The English Street screen & session flow

**Zone screen.** A side-scrolling street. Unlocked buildings are colored; locked ones are grey shadows with a "?" (a "who's coming?" hook). The 🚢 ship sits at the start of the street. Tapping a building plays its game, and a **big ▶ "Drive with Zip"** button starts the guided session.

**"Drive with Zip": a 6–8 minute guided session** (so he doesn't have to choose):
1. Zip says hello (and invites a *"Hello!"* back).
2. 🚢 **Ship Arrives**: meet up to 3 new words.
3. 🔊 **Zip's Garage**: 5 hear-and-tap rounds (new words + review).
4. One "action" game picked by the unit (Zip Says, Fix-it Zip, Paint it!, Count…), 5 rounds.
5. 🎵 The unit's chant, once.
6. Word cards earned, then *"Bye bye! See you tomorrow!"*.

The session limit and sleep screen apply as usual.

## 7. The games

Every game follows the house rules: ≤4 big choices, a 🔊 replay button, wobble on a miss, a glow after 2 misses, the answer revealed after 3, a sticker or word card after 5 rounds, and adaptive levels. **Level** (difficulty of the task) and **stage** (how much Vietnamese help a *word* gets) are separate: a level 3 game can still give stage-A help for a brand-new word.

| 2 | 🔊 Zip's Garage `engFind` | choice | listening, nouns | 2 choices | 3 | 4, with **adjective + noun** ("the red bus", "the white Toyota") | 1 |
| 3 | 🚦 Zip Says `engDo` | custom | actions (TPR) | Zip says **go / stop**, he taps it (new word: meaning + glowing button) | + slow, honk | + fast, lights (built); later: up, down, turn, two-step | 1 |

### Game details

**1 · Ship Arrives `engMeet`.** A ship's horn sounds, a container opens and a vehicle drives out (or a color splashes onto Zip, or Zip *does* an action). The word is said with the sandwich, and he can tap the picture as many times as he likes to hear it again. A 2-choice check ends each word ("Where's the **fire truck**?"). This is the only place new words enter.

**2 · Zip's Garage `engFind`.** The workhorse: a garage door rolls up showing 2–4 vehicles, Zip asks for one, and he taps it. The answer is said in English with the vehicle's sound ("**Fire truck!** *nee-naw*"). L3 adds the adjective, which is where color and size words get used again.

**3 · Zip Says `engDo`.** Zip on a road with big icon buttons (green arrow = go, stop sign, turtle = slow, chevrons = fast, horn, headlight). **Every level is "Zip says a word, he taps it":** Zip says one English action word (*"go!"*), the buttons wait until it's said, he taps that button and Zip does it. While a word is new the helper adds the meaning (*"Zip nói: go! Nghĩa là chạy!"*) and the right button glows, so he learns which picture goes with the word; once known, Zip says just the word. A wrong button wobbles, Zip doesn't move, and the word is said again. (An earlier free-play L1 was removed: with no word to follow it taught nothing.) There are **no "Simon says" trick commands**: being caught out frustrates children this age.

**4 · Fix-it Zip `engParts`.** *"Uh-oh! The wheel is broken!"* A part blinks grey, and he taps that part on a big drawing (it uses the same `data-region` hit test as Paint Shop). A wrench sound plays, the part gets fixed, and Zip says *"Thank you!"*. The region names map straight from `art/defaults.ts`: `wheels, window, lights, siren, ladder, bucket, arm, hook, rocks, drum, tracks`.

**5 · Paint it! `engPaint`.** One car and 2–4 paint pots. *"Paint the bus **blue**!"* Tapping the right pot pours paint over the bus. There's also a **setting for the regular Paint Shop and Drawing Board to say colors in English** (§9), which costs almost nothing to build.

**6 · Count in English `engCount`.** The existing *Count the Cars* generator, with numbers said in English. At L1 each tap says both: *"một — **one**"*. The answer comes back as a chunk with the plural: *"**Three cars!**"*.

**7 · Order Up `engOrder`.** Build a Car's L2/L3 customer orders, spoken by the customer in English: *"Hello! A **yellow** **taxi**, please!"* → *"Thank you! Bye bye!"*. This teaches **please / thank you / hello / bye** through use.

**8 · Opposites Road `engOpposites`.** Short animations: two cars race (*fast / slow*), the crane hook moves (*up / down*), dump trucks (*full / empty*), a car goes through the car wash (*dirty → clean*). *"Which car is **fast**?"*

**9 · Where's Zip? `engWhere`.** 2–4 picture cards, each showing Zip in a different place relative to a garage, bridge or ramp. *"Zip is **under** the bridge!"* He taps the matching picture.

**10 · Sing & Drive `engSong`.** Spoken chants over a simple synthesized beat (`sound.ts`). Text-to-speech can't sing, so the chants work best recorded by Dad. Chants are in §10.

**11 · Say it to Zip `engSay`.** It uses the microphone for **loudness only**: no speech recognition, nothing recorded or stored, nothing leaves the device. *"Say GO and Zip will go!"* Any voice above a threshold for ~0.3 s makes Zip zoom off. It **rewards trying, not pronunciation**. It's off by default, a grown-up turns it on, and iOS asks for the mic once.

## 8. Classmates: friends he names, who play along inside every game

Classmates aren't a separate place or activity. They're **game characters who ride along in every English Street game**. Two little friends sit in their small cars at the edge of the screen. He gives them names, and during the game they **talk, cheer, joke, ask for his help, take a turn now and then, and react to everything he does**. He can tap them at any time to make them do something funny. The aim is to make the games *more fun*: he's never playing alone, and his friends are always on his side.

### Meeting and naming them

They arrive like everything else on English Street: **off the ship** (in *Ship Arrives*, the first time he plays).
1. A small car rolls off the ship, honks and waves. Zip: *"Hello! What's my name?"*
2. **He names the friend himself**, three ways (built):
   - 🎤 **Say it:** he taps the mic and says any name (3 s). His recording *becomes* the name: wherever the friend's name comes up, his own voice is played inside the sentence (*"Tớ là [his voice]! Chào King!"*). Silence → "say it again, louder". The clip is a small audio file kept with the friend on this device. Browsers allow the mic only on https or localhost; elsewhere the mic button hides itself.
   - 🔤 **Tap letters:** a big A–Z keyboard (up to 8 letters, ⌫), each letter said aloud; any letters work, so silly names are part of the fun.
   - 🎲 **Ready names:** 4 chips said aloud when tapped (*Bin, Na, Su, Bo, Mia, Tom…*, short names that sound right in both voices).

   The friend says the name straight back: *"I'm Bin! Hi, {kid}!"*
3. He picks the friend's **car** and **color** from words he already knows: *"What's Bin's car? → A fire truck! What color? → Blue!"*. Naming a friend is word practice too.

A grown-up can also type any name in settings, such as a cousin, a sibling or a real friend, with a 🔊 *Test* button and a *"spoken as"* field for names the English voice says badly (like `spoken` for MG in `brands.ts`). Names stay on the device.

Two friends join at the start. A third comes off the ship when unit 4 opens and a fourth at unit 7, each with a new naming moment.

### Who they are

They're **animal kids** (bear, cat, dog and a new bunny), reusing the Build a Car driver art. Each has its own voice (pitch and speed) and **one personality**, so he always knows what to expect from whom:

| Default | Personality | Typical moments |
|---|---|---|
| 🐻 Bear | **Silly, sleepy** | Gets things funnily wrong for him to fix (*"Is THIS the fire truck?"* while holding up a bus). Falls asleep and snores when nobody has tapped anything for a while. |
| 🐱 Cat | **Curious** | Spots surprises: *"Look! What's that?"*. Asks *"What's this?"* about things in the scene. |
| 🐶 Dog | **Speedy, loud** | Cheers the loudest, does wheelies, says *"Ready, set, go!"* and *"High five!"*. |
| 🐰 Bunny | **Shy, sweet** | Gets stuck and whispers *"Can you help me?"*, then says the word proudly once he helps. |

### What they do during a game

These hooks work in **every** English game automatically. A game only sends events (start, right, miss, hint, streak, idle, end), and the friends react.

| When | What a friend does | Example | What he does |

| a friend needs help (~1 round in 5) | the shy friend asks | Bunny: **"Where is the… um… crane? Can you help me?"** | taps the crane → Bunny: **"Crane! Thank you!"** |

Beep!"**) · **"I like blue!"** · **"My car is fast!"** · mix-up jokes: **"A car says… moo!"** → he taps **No!** → **"Hee hee! A car says beep beep!"** | after 3 taps in a row: **"Let's play! Your turn!"** steers him back to the game |

### Making them his

- **Dress-up:** each new word card earns a little extra (spoiler, siren, flag, roof rack or taxi sign from Build a Car, plus hats). He chooses: *"Give Bin a spoiler!"*. The friend wears it everywhere.
- **They remember:** the color he painted them, the extra he gave them, and how many fire trucks he found today (*"{kid}, you found 3 fire trucks!"*).
- **Paint Shop pages:** each friend's car is a page in the coloring book.

### Rules that keep them fun

- **He's the hero.** Friends never beat him, never score points and never get it right "first". A friend's mistake is always a joke that *he* gets to fix.
- **One friend talks at a time**, in short lines (≤2 s), with at most one comment per round on the default setting. His tap always cuts them off.
- **Friends never block the game.** Tapping them is always optional, and turns and help requests are skipped at L1.
- **Nothing mean.** Nobody laughs at a mistake, including his friends'. *"Silly me!"* is the friend laughing at themself.

### Settings → Classmates

**On/off** · **how many: 1–4** · **names**: edit, 🔊 test, "spoken as" · **silly mistakes on/off** · **chattiness: quiet** (only cheers) **/ normal / chatty**. The friends are built as a generic overlay, so later they could also ride along in the other zones (a separate switch).

## 9. English everywhere: echo in the existing games (cheap, high value)

This is a Grown-ups setting: **"English echo: off / words / words + counting"**. When it's on and the app language is Vietnamese:
- **Correct answers echo the English word** in every existing game: *"Đúng rồi! Xe cứu hỏa — **fire truck**!"* (color names, vehicle names, numbers, shapes).
- **Paint Shop / Drawing Board** say each color in both languages: *"Màu đỏ — **red**!"*.
- **Count the Cars / Transporter** can count in English after Vietnamese.
- **Stickers** are announced in both: *"… a **red fire truck**!"*.

This turns all 26 existing games into extra English exposure, which is principle 4, and needs only the per-line language switch from §11.

## 10. Script

Lines marked **Zip** are always English (en-US voice, pitch 1.3). **Helper** lines are in the app language (shown in Vietnamese here, with English for when the app is in English). `{w}` is the English target word, `{wv}` the Vietnamese one, `{kid}` the child's name. ⏸ = pause (ms). These go into `en.ts` / `vi.ts` under `eng.*` keys once built.

## 11. Engineering plan

All of this fits the current architecture. The changes, by file:

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| `engine/i18n.ts` | `Line` gets an optional `lang?: Lang`. `t()` uses `line.lang ?? lang` for that line, and nested args inherit it. Add helper `EN(key, args?)` = `{ ...L(key, args), lang: 'en' }`. |
| `engine/speech.ts` | `say()` uses `step.lang ?? state.lang` for the voice tag, the recording lookup (`en/<key>.mp3`) and the text. Add optional `rate`/`pitch` on a step (Zip = pitch 1.3; target words = rate × 0.85). **This one change unlocks everything else.** |
| `content/english.ts` *(new)* | `WORDS: EngWord[] = { id, unit, kind: 'vehicle'│'color'│'number'│'action'│'part'│'describe'│'where'│'job'│'phrase', en: key, vi: key, thing: Thing, sfx? }`. ## 13. A 4-week start (after Phase 1)

| Week | In the app (≈8 min/day, "Drive with Zip") | Off screen (≈5 min, a grown-up) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unit 1: car, bus, fire truck, police car, ambulance · Zip Says go/stop | On the road: "Look! A **bus**!" · toy cars at bath time: "**Go!** … **Stop!**" |
| 2 | Unit 1 review + unit 2 construction | Point out diggers and trucks on the street in English · "Bye bye, **excavator**!" |
| 3 | Unit 3 colors (echo on in Paint Shop) | "I spy a **red** car!" on car rides · he picks a toy car "by color" |
| 4 | Units 1–3 mixed, chants 1–3 | Ask him to **teach you** a word: "How do you say *xe buýt* to Zip?" |

**Signs it's working** (grown-ups log them with "He said it! ✓"): after 4 weeks he recognizes about 20–25 words (box ≥3) and follows *go / stop*. After 3 months he recognizes ~60–80 words, follows two-step commands, and **says 10+ English words on his own** (often *"Beep beep! Go!"*, colors and vehicle names first). Expect understanding to run well ahead of speaking. That's normal.

## 14. Open questions for a grown-up

- **Voice**: record Zip's lines with a real person (more charming, and unit chants need it) or keep TTS for Zip and record only the chants?
- **"excavator" or "digger"?** The app uses US English (excavator). Teach *digger* as a second name at L3?
- **Mic game**: worth the permission prompt, or keep speaking practice off-screen only?
- **Classmates**: animal kids (the default, reusing the driver art) or human kids? Should the name chips be English-style (Mia, Tom), Vietnamese nicknames that work in both voices (Bin, Na, Su), or a mix?
